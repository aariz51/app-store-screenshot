## Universal principles (seen in 5–6 of 6)
1. **One idea per tile**, with a headline of 2–6 words. [OBS·H]
2. **Headline in the top or bottom third, never the middle.** The device takes the other two thirds. [OBS·H]
3. **The device is cropped** (top or bottom bleed) so the screen can be larger than a full phone would allow. Seen in every reference. [OBS·H]
4. **Real UI first.** Every reference shows actual app screens; the decoration supports them and never replaces them. [OBS·H]
5. **Colour discipline**: 1 brand colour + 1 neutral + at most 1 extra accent. [MEAS·H]
6. **Heavy display weight** (ExtraBold to Black) for all headlines, so they stay readable at search-result thumbnail size (about 1/6 of the original). [OBS·H]
7. **Brand-coloured key word** to give a two-level read within the headline. [OBS·H, in 5 of 6]
8. **Something breaks the frame** (a card wider than the phone, an object crossing the tile edge). It adds depth and keeps the tile from looking like a template. [OBS·H, in 6 of 6]

## Unique patterns
- A: **cross-tile panorama** with a giant rotated phone; **magnified-callout cards** as the main focal device.
- B: **mascot as presenter**; glyph substitution (coins as letters); word-level size jumps.
- C: **highlight band** across the screen; magnifier lens; fanned 3D file icons.
- D: **zig-zag top/bottom rhythm**; masks instead of faces; neon glow instead of shadow.
- E: **bento tiles instead of devices**; numbered steps; user-generated content as the colour source.
- F: **before/after with numbers** in the hero tile; hand-drawn underline; speech-bubble chips.

## Reusable for any app
Magnified callouts (A) · top/bottom zig-zag (D) · two-tone key word (A, D, E) · bento for multi-input features (E) · numbered steps for workflow tools (E) · quantified before/after when the product has a measurable outcome (F) · frame-breaking element (all).

## Category-specific (use only when it fits)
- Mascot, coins, stickers (B): gamified, kids, habit, education. Fails for finance, health-clinical, B2B.
- Neon on black (D): nightlife, dating, music, gaming, privacy. Fails for family, kids, health.
- ALL-CAPS condensed shouting (C): utilities with one clear job. Fails for premium, calm or wellness apps.
- Hand-held lifestyle photo (A6): consumer lifestyle. Remember the no-faces rule (hands only).
- Laurels and ratings (A, C, F): **only with verifiable facts**.

## Conflicts with a strict house layout contract (example)
| Contract says | References do | Resolution |
|---|---|---|
| No connector arrows or pointer lines | C and F use arrows | Default: no arrows. Allowed only under Playful/Utility archetypes **when the owner opts in**. |
| No sticker packs | B and C use stickers / starbursts | Same: off by default; Playful archetype only, with opt-in. |
| No human faces | B5 person, E1 photo faces | Always replace with masks (D), hands, silhouettes, mascots, or objects. |
| One huge headline, single line where possible | A, D, E use 2–3 lines | Prefer 1–2 lines; 3 lines only for condensed caps. |
| Callout cards show the complete UI section | A does this exactly | Matches. A is the reference implementation. |
| App/brand name at the top | Only F shows the brand lockup (on tile 1) | Contract wins: brand name on tile 1 at minimum. |
